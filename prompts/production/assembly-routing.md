## Nine connection classes

Every supplied connection has exactly one immutable `connectionMode`. Apply these meanings without substitution:

1. `board-mount`: a module/header is seated in a carrier/socket. It is a deterministic rigid transform and never a rendered wire.
2. `rigid-mate`: an intact keyed plug, servo plug, socket, terminal plug, or housed connector seats onto its compatible target. Its electrical contacts remain in the netlist, but those contacts are never emitted as separate jumper wires.
3. `factory-harness`: flexible conductors are permanently attached to a selected device and terminate in the selected intact plug. Only the explicitly deformable free span may be shaped. Preserve device exit, usable length, conductor order, color, diameter, minimum bend radius, plug ownership, and plug contact order.
4. `deformable-factory-harness`: a hash-bound existing cable or included independent wire has two compiler-seated physical terminations and one compiler-owned free span. Preserve both rigid mates, both wire-exit anchors, measured length ceiling, diameter, minimum bend radius, bundle identity, and hidden baked-conductor source node. Render one open runtime tube and no loose substitute.
5. `deformable-servo-harness`: one selected cable asset keeps its three-contact servo plug intact while its black GND, red regulated POWER, and yellow PWM conductors run from compiler-fitted servo-side wire exits to three compiler-fitted source terminations. Preserve the single servo-side three-contact rigid transform, each source-side rigid transform, conductor order, separate output-channel ownership, usable length, physical diameter, minimum bend radius, and bundle identity. GND and POWER terminate only on the selected regulated power/distribution asset; PWM terminates only on the selected legal PWM surface contact. The separately supplied common-ground bond remains one routed conductor between the controller/carrier ground and the regulated power system; do not duplicate it once per servo.
6. `deformable-powered-logic-harness`: one selected six-conductor cable asset preserves an intact keyed device mate and connects a compiler-selected regulated/translated interface system to the device and controller surface. Preserve all twelve rigid mates and the six immutable roles: red DEVICE_POWER, black DEVICE_GROUND, yellow DEVICE_SIGNAL_HIGH, black SURFACE_GROUND, red SURFACE_LOGIC_SUPPLY, and yellow SURFACE_SIGNAL_LOW. The high-side and low-side signal are different nets separated by the proven translator; never join them, bypass the interface system, reverse direction, or route the high-side voltage to the controller. Preserve each transform group, wire-exit anchor, length, diameter, bend radius, bundle identity, and exact device contact order.
7. `deformable-exact-mating-cable`: one catalog cable preserves a keyed multi-contact device plug and exposes only its declared opposite-end terminations. Preserve the full ordered keyed contact row, one grouped device-side transform, independent far-end transforms, two rigid mates for every routed conductor, each immutable wire-exit anchor, usable length, diameter, bend radius, and bundle identity. Route only `routedSignals`. A declared `unmatedSignal` such as NC retains both physical cable contacts but receives no electrical edge and no controller/carrier mate. A declared opposite-end strap such as SEL→GND is one real conductor whose device signal is SEL and whose destination net/contact is GND; never omit it or turn NC into a spare GPIO.
8. `routed-conductor`: one accepted logical guide edge between two explicitly routable contacts. Both endpoint nodes must already exist. The guide projection renders only the external component-contact to carrier-contact line; it renders no sleeve, plug, or jumper product. Grove may use this presentation class only when the compiler has already validated the exact `logicalGuideAccessoryRequirements` cable SKU, two plug mates, straight-through four-position order, sensor receptacle, and XIAO-base receptacle; this changes presentation only and never turns Grove into loose jumpers. The class remains forbidden for unresolved Grove, Qwiic/JST-SH, JST-PH/GH/XH, servo plugs, factory-baked leads, battery connectors, hook clips, or any incomplete exact-cable, harness, servo, or powered-logic contract. The sole servo-plug exception is a compiler-accepted connection carrying `ownerVerifiedServoGuide=true` and `ownerVerifiedCarrierPowerRuleId=owner-bench-fs90r-s3-carrier-gvs-v3`; preserve those exact endpoints and colors without inventing cable geometry. Every v3 channel owns one complete carrier GVS row: a unique black GND contact, unique red 3V3 positive-supply contact, and unique yellow PWM contact sharing the same bank and row signal. Reject any duplicate carrier endpoint, partial or mixed row, shared-pin fan-in, or same-net crossing waiver.
9. `logical-guide`: the deterministic compiler's final, electrically accepted pin-to-pin projection for this circuit-only product. It is a valid ready connection class, not a missing cable type and not an instruction to guess endpoints. Preserve its exact endpoints, net, route samples, and wire ID. ## Natural wire shape

- Every conductor is one open, continuous path from its exact source contact to its exact destination contact.
- Prefer the shortest collision-free route that still has a small amount of believable slack: a short connector-normal lead-out, one broad free-span arch, and a short connector-normal lead-in.
- Use at most one broad apex. The route should make steady overall progress from source to destination after its endpoint fan-out.
- Never request a service loop, coil, circle, closed turn, knot, braid, helix, self-intersection, repeated traversal, doubled-back section, decorative sweep, or cable wrapped around a component.
- Never request a giant U, C, S, rectangle, square, Manhattan path, perimeter trace, or route that outlines a board, part, enclosure, or scene boundary. Do not send a cable far away merely to bring it back.
- Never use rigid plumbing-like elbows, hard 90-degree corners, long straight rectangular detours, or polygonal zigzags. Bends must be broad, smooth, and tangent-continuous.
- Select the smallest permitted bow height and lane offset that preserve separation and clearance. Do not choose the schema maximum for drama. Never create an arch substantially larger than the endpoint span.
- Treat every coordinate and physical dimension as metres in one immutable world frame. Never request, assume, or compensate for a display-only scene scale. The camera and renderer must consume the same unscaled physical frame as parts, endpoints, keepouts, mates, and routes.
- For an unobstructed span, keep the bow height at or below the smaller of 18 mm and 25% of endpoint distance. Use a higher bow only when the deterministic keepout solver proves that exact additional rise is required; a decorative tent-pole arch is forbidden.
- Keep total routed length compact: normally no more than 1.35 times the direct connector-to-connector distance, excluding only the minimum connector-normal engagement and a contract-owned fixed factory-lead segment. Any larger ratio must be explained by a named intervening keepout and still may not double back.
- If a short, smooth, collision-free arch is impossible, return blocked for that wire. A large rectangular detour or loop is never an acceptable fallback.

## Logical guide-line appearance

- The deterministic route must leave and enter along the supplied connector normals. Request no side-entry shortcut through a board or connector housing.
- Each guide centerline must terminate exactly at the external component contact and exact carrier contact. It may not stop short, float above the board, touch only a decorative surface, terminate on an omitted cable part, or disappear into the wrong side.
- Preserve exact factory cable geometry, connector order, and conductor order only as hidden electrical evidence. Do not render its jacket, plug, ribbon, sleeves, or accessory body.
- Request a thin, smooth, round graphic guide line: clearly visible but visually lighter than the GLB parts, never a realistic cable, hairline, flat ribbon, oversized hose, pipe, wall, or extruded rectangular bar.
- Neighboring conductors may share a gentle corridor, but each must remain individually traceable. Keep stable lane order, modest even spacing, and similar curvature. Never twist, weave, cross, or braid them.
- Never request or preserve one progressively taller height layer per wire. All bundles begin in one shallow clearance band and receive only a collision-proven local lift; a staircase, cage, or tall rectangular fan-out is invalid.
- Request one smooth open arch per guide. The deterministic geometry is two sampled cubic Bézier spans joined at one apex with a continuous tangent and connector-normal endpoint tangents. For an underside-facing termination, the compiler first clears the PCB edge below the board and inserts a radius-gated tangent circular transition into the main arch; Sol must preserve that supplied transition and may not pull the guide through the PCB or force a tight U-turn at the pin. Never request a vertical riser plus flat roof, rectangular plumbing, stepped conduit, squared shoulder, or long constant-height plateau.
- Preserve electrical color semantics supplied by the contract. When the contract explicitly delegates default colors, use black for GND, red for positive power, and yellow for signal or I/O. - A blocked or superseded request must atomically clear every prior part, wire, connection row, assembly step, count, and machine gate. Late GLB loads from an older request must be discarded and may never repopulate the current scene.

## Clearance, readability, and composition

- Cables must not pass through, clip, graze, or hide inside any PCB, controller, sensor, carrier, connector, pin row, or other supplied keepout mesh.
- Treat the deterministic renderer's padded world-space bounds for every resolved GLB as hard solids. Every complete free-span line or curve segment and the full conductor radius must remain outside every non-endpoint solid; checking only sampled vertices is forbidden because a segment can otherwise tunnel through a thin part. Touching a bound is a collision, not a pass.
- The only permitted entry into a source or destination keepout is the short, contract-owned connector engagement segment between the exact contact and its deterministic outward escape point. After that escape point, the route must remain outside the source/destination GLBs too.
- Rise above the tallest intervening padded GLB before crossing its XY footprint. Maintain at least the supplied production clearance above that highest keepout; never skim a component top, antenna can, pin row, sensor package, or connector shell.
- Collision clearance is symmetric: check the entire cable radius, not only its mathematical centerline. Parallel conductors need both mesh clearance and conductor-to-conductor spacing.
- Favor a clear corridor around obstacles while remaining compact. Use a modest change of bow side or lane only; never solve an obstacle by circling it or tracing its perimeter.
- Keep endpoints, color identity, and the full source-to-destination path readable from the supplied assembly view without changing electrical placement.
